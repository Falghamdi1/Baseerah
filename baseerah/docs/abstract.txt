BASEERAH (بصيرة) — AI SIMULATION COPILOT FOR FUTURE RESOURCE OPTIMIZATION
PSU AI Hackathon 2.0 | May 2026
Author: Faisal Alghamdi | College of Computer & Information Sciences | Prince Sultan University

ABSTRACT
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Saudi households lose thousands of riyals every year to three invisible resource
drains: food that spoils before it is eaten, electricity consumed in inefficient
patterns, and water used far above sustainable levels. The true cost of these
habits only becomes visible months later — by which point behavioral change feels
difficult and the financial damage is already done. Existing tools either monitor
consumption passively or present raw numbers that users cannot act on. What is
missing is not data — it is consequence.

Baseerah (بصيرة), meaning "insight" in Arabic, is an AI-powered simulation
copilot that makes the future cost of daily habits visible before it happens.
The system asks users plain, everyday questions — "How long are your showers?",
"How often does food go to waste?", "How many hours is your AC on?" — and
instantly simulates three parallel futures: a Current path with no changes, an
Optimized path using practical smart habits, and an Aggressive path for maximum
savings. All three scenarios run simultaneously across four time horizons:
3 months, 6 months, 1 year, and 3 years.

The core innovation is a translation layer that converts friendly user answers
into calibrated model parameters without exposing any technical values. Under
the hood, three physics-based models run the simulation. The electricity model
uses linear regression to derive monthly kWh consumption from AC runtime,
appliance usage, and laundry frequency, then applies a time-of-use pricing
uplift for peak-hour households and a Saudi seasonal overlay. The food waste
model compounds four factors — fridge temperature spoilage, meal-prep
exponential decay, weekend spike patterns, and deterministic noise — to produce
weekly waste projections in SAR. The water model decomposes household
consumption into components (shower duration at 9 L/min, laundry at 65 L/load,
irrigation, and general use) and reduces each independently per strategy.

The system is built on a FastAPI backend with Pydantic validation and a Vanilla
JavaScript frontend with Chart.js visualizations. All constants are grounded in
published data from the Saudi Electricity Company (SEC), the National Water
Company (NWC), the Saudi Energy Efficiency Center (SEEC), the Food and
Agriculture Organization (FAO), and the World Health Organization (WHO). The
application runs fully offline — opening the HTML file in any browser launches
a complete simulation without a server.

Results validated against the demo profile (Ahmed, Riyadh, household of 3) show
that Optimized mode achieves a 25% reduction in electricity, 38% reduction in
food waste, and 22% reduction in water usage, saving approximately SAR 10,500
over three years with no hardware purchases and no major lifestyle changes. An
AI insights engine detects behavioral patterns — weekend waste spikes, peak-hour
electricity draw, excessive shower duration — and ranks them by financial impact,
generating specific actionable recommendations for each.

A Campus Mode toggle scales the same simulation by 300 university residential
units, transforming a household dashboard into an institution-level sustainability
decision tool — projecting annual campus savings of SAR 3.1 million under
optimized operation.

Baseerah demonstrates that the barrier to sustainable behavior is not motivation
— it is visibility. When people see their future in SAR, they change.
